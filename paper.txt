Towards trustworthy lithium-ion battery remaining useful life prediction
with uncertainty quantification

Zhibin Zhao a , Mingqi Feng a, Qiuyu Yu a, Hongming Yuan a, Fujin Wang a,b ,, Zhi Zhai a,

ARTICLE INFO                ABSTRACT

Keywords:                   With the rapid growth of electric vehicles and renewable energy storage systems, lithium-ion batteries have
Remaining useful life       become a cornerstone of modern energy infrastructure, making accurate and trustworthy remaining useful life
Uncertainty quantification  (RUL) prediction crucial for operational safety. However, most existing data-driven models predominantly
Deep evidential regression  focus on deterministic point estimates while overlooking the underlying prediction uncertainty, which
Lithium-ion battery         severely undermines the trustworthiness of RUL predictions in safety-critical applications. This paper proposes
Trustworthiness             a Sparsely-gated Mixture of Experts Long Short-Term Memory (SMoE-LSTM) model for trustworthy RUL
                            prediction. The model integrates a sparsely gated mixture of experts with LSTM networks to adaptively
                            capture complex, multi-stage degradation dynamics. The model further incorporates deep evidential regression.
                            This allows for the simultaneous output of RUL estimates and decoupled uncertainties in a single forward
                            propagation. Experimental validation on the MIT-Stanford dataset demonstrates that the proposed method
                            achieves an average RMSE of 16.11 and an R2 of 0.9821, outperforming baseline methods in both accuracy and
                            uncertainty calibration. Moreover, its single-pass inference design provides substantially higher computational
                            efficiency than MC Dropout and ensemble methods, making it more suitable for real-time battery management
                            systems.
